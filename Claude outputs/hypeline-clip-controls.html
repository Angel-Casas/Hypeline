Option 1

One line of dropdowns

measuring…

The three pill groups become three small menus that show their current value. Presets keep their pills, because one tap is the point of a preset. Captions and thumbnail move into a menu of their own. Everything stays one click away, nothing is hidden behind a mode.

Option 2

Preset, then a summary you can open

measuring…

A preset already decides shape, cut and size, so most of the time those three rows are just restating what the preset said. Show them as a read-only summary with an "Adjust" link; the full controls drop down when someone wants them and stay open for the rest of the session.

Option 3

A sentence you can edit

measuring…

The settings read as one line of English, each underlined word a menu. It states what will happen rather than listing switches, and it is the only option that also explains itself to a first-time user. Risk: prose wraps differently in ten languages, so it needs care in German and Japanese.

Option 4

Two columns, nothing hidden

measuring…

No menus at all: two labelled columns, so the empty space at the end of every row becomes the next row instead. Everything stays visible and one tap away — the honest option if you would rather not trade clicks for pixels. Below about 900 px it falls back to today's single stack, so this only helps the desktop, which is where the problem is.

Option 5

Settings scroll, the export bar never does

measuring…

Leave the controls exactly as they are, but give them a bounded scroll area and pin the export row to the bottom of the panel. The fold stops mattering: whatever you scroll to, Export is under your cursor. Scroll the box below to feel it. This is the one that pairs with the moments list fix, since it is the same idea applied twice.

Also asked for

The moments list

Whichever of the five you pick, the moments list gets the same treatment independently: a bounded height with its own scrollbar once it passes about six moments, so a sensitive VOD with twenty peaks stops pushing the clip panel down the page. On a phone it stays as it is — there, the page scroll is the right scroll.

  • 1 — Dropdowns. Biggest saving, keeps every control reachable, costs a click per change.
  • 2 — Summary. Rewards the preset workflow; the fiddly controls are there when wanted.
  • 3 — Sentence. The most characterful, the most work to translate well.
  • 4 — Two columns. No new interaction to learn, a middling saving, desktop only.
  • 5 — Pinned bar. Doesn't shrink anything; fixes the panel's height so the fold stops mattering.
