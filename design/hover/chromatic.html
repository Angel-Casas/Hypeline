Hypeline · hover

A chromatic surface instead of an inversion

The hovered thing becomes a sheet of metal rather than swapping with the page: white through silver by day, black through a deep blue by night. The text never changes colour, which is the real prize — no nested control can disappear into the fill, and nothing has to be told to invert with its parent. Every option uses the same ramp, built the way the silk ring is: mirrored, so it can drift without a seam, and with no flat stretch, because a flat silver is just grey — it is the gradient that makes it read as material.

Option 1

Metal, wiped in

the sweep you chose, in silver

Exactly the motion that is in the app now — the fill grows from the left over 220 ms — with the ink swapped for the metal. Because the text stays put, this is the cheapest of the lot to own: no descendant rules, no re-inversion for nested buttons, nothing to remember. By night the sheet is black-to-blue, which on the true-black page reads as the surface catching a light rather than lighting up.

POGGERSemote19
Option 2

Metal from the middle

opens outwards instead of across

The same sheet and the same 220 ms, opening outwards from the centre rather than sweeping across. It has no direction, so a column of rows does not feel like it is being read left to right, and a small round control opens like an iris. (A true cross-fade is the one thing this mechanism cannot do — a background image cannot fade — so it would cost a pseudo-element, which is exactly what we moved off last night.)

POGGERSemote19
Option 3

Living metal

keeps drifting while you hover

The sheet wipes in and then never settles: the ramp drifts for as long as the pointer stays, at the speed the silk button uses. The most alive of the six and the one most obviously related to the rest of the app — and the one to be most careful with, since a long list of rows would each start moving as you pass over them.

POGGERSemote19
Option 4

One shine

a highlight crosses once

The surface fills, and then a single specular band travels across it and is gone. Motion that happens once per hover rather than continuously, so it reads as a reaction rather than an animation — the closest of the six to picking something up and catching the light on it.

POGGERSemote19
Option 5

Iridescent

silver with the palette left in

The same construction, but the greys are tinted — a little lilac, a little sky, a little petal, all far too pale to read as colour on their own. It is the version that could not possibly be mistaken for a default UI hover, and the one that risks looking dirty if the tints are pushed a step further.

POGGERSemote19
Option 6

Metal and a lift

fills, and rises 1 px

Option 1 plus a single pixel of height and a shadow. It makes small controls feel like objects, and it would let one tier serve where there are currently two — hover-lift and hover-invert would become the same gesture at different strengths.

Pick a number and it becomes the one hover in the app — rows, menu items, icon buttons, ghost buttons, chips, links. hover-danger stays red, and hover-line (fields) stays an ink edge. Two things to decide with it: whether the chip and pack borders stay ink now that they are not inverting, and whether hover-lift survives as its own tier or folds into option 6.
