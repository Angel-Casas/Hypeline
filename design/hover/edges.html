Hypeline · hover · round two

Eight more, in the family you liked

Both of the ones you kept answer with material rather than colour — an edge that lights up, and glass becoming more like glass. So these eight are all light, edge, texture and depth; none of them fills the surface with a colour. The two keepers are at the top, marked, so you can hover them side by side with the new ones. Each panel has the app's mesh behind it, because frost and glow need something to work on.

A

Both at once

silk edge + frost

The obvious first question: the glass thickens and the silk ring appears around it, replacing the hairline. The two do different jobs — the frost says "this surface is active", the ring says "this one, specifically" — and together they are the loudest thing in this file without ever filling anything.

B

One turn of the rim

a light runs around the edge, once

The border is a dark hairline with one bright arc in it, and on hover that arc travels all the way around and stops. Same machinery as the silk ring, but it reads as a rim catching light rather than as a rainbow — and because it happens once, a list of these does not turn into a disco.

C

Light from above

an inner highlight and a warm pool

Nothing outside the element changes. A bright line appears along its top edge and a very faint warm light pools beneath it, as though a lamp had been turned on above the desk. Closest in spirit to frost, but it works even where there is nothing behind to blur — which is most of the panels in this app.

D

A halo

the glow is outside, not inside

A soft lilac ring sits just off the edge with a petal-coloured bloom behind it. The element is untouched; the space around it changes. Good on a dark page, and the one option here that would still be visible from the corner of your eye.

E

The grain blooms

the paper texture, on this one thing

The whole app sits on a faint paper grain. Here the grain gets denser on the hovered element only, so it turns into a piece of card lying on the page. No colour, no movement, no light — a change of substance. It is the subtlest in this file, and the most likely to be missed on a small control.

F

It tilts towards you

follows the pointer in 3D

The element tips a couple of degrees towards wherever the pointer is, like a card held under a light. Physical and quite playful; it needs a line of JavaScript for the pointer position, and it is the one option here that could feel like too much on a long list of rows.

G

The mesh wakes under it

the background blooms through

The app's drifting pastel mesh is always behind everything, usually far too soft to see. On hover a patch of it swells up behind the element and fades again. The colour is the app's own atmosphere rather than a new hover colour — and because it is blurred and outside the box, it never touches the text.

H

The hairline thickens

1 px becomes 2, in ink

The quietest possible answer: the border that is already there simply gets heavier and darker. No new colour, no new geometry, nothing to learn. Worth having in the file as the floor — if one of the others is not clearly better than this, it is not worth its complexity.
