Hypeline · hover

Seven hovers, mostly inversions

Every option below is applied to the real elements it would affect: a “seen in this VOD” row, its + important button, a ghost button, an icon button, a starter pack (silk ring and all) and an underlined link. Hover them. Switch themes with the button — an inversion means something different in each, which is the whole question.

Today

The accent wash

what you have now

A violet film at 11 %. It is not grey, but it is not much of anything either — and on the pack chips it fights the silk ring.

Option 1

Straight inversion

ink fills · paper text

The element swaps with the page: ink background, ground-coloured text. By night that is near-white on black becoming black on near-white, so it inverts twice as hard — the strongest signal of the seven, and the most obviously “pressable”. The cost is weight: a list of rows flashes a solid black bar under the pointer.

POGGERSemote19
Option 2

Warm inversion

apricot fills · ink text

The same swap, but the fill is the apricot from the warm silk rather than ink. Ink text stays ink, so only the background flips — it reads as the element lighting up rather than going dark. Identical in both themes, which is rare and worth something, and it ties the hover to the rings. Risk: apricot is also --pick-soft, so hover and “chosen” live next door.

POGGERSemote19
Option 3

Silk inversion

the warm ramp fills · ink text

Option 2 with the whole butter → apricot ramp instead of one flat hue, so the hovered thing is briefly made of the same material as the rings. The most “Hypeline” of the seven. On a wide row it is a lot of gradient; on small controls it is lovely.

POGGERSemote19
Option 4

Wipe inversion

ink arrives from the left

Option 1, but the ink sweeps in from the left edge over 220 ms instead of appearing. The motion is the point: it makes even a quiet control feel answered, and the direction gives a row the same reading direction as its text. Under reduced motion it would simply appear, which is option 1.

POGGERSemote19
Option 5

Half inversion

ink fills · butter text

The background inverts but the text lands on a palette hue rather than the paper — butter by day, violet by night. Inversion with a colour left in it. It is the most distinctive and the least neutral; on a long list it starts to feel like a theme of its own.

POGGERSemote19
Option 6

Paper inversion

the lightest thing fills

The mirror of option 1: instead of the darkest value filling, the lightest does — pure white with an ink hairline by day, the near-white ink colour by night. By day it is the quietest of all the inversions (the page is already nearly white, so only the edge and the crispness change); by night it is as loud as option 1. Handy if the black bar in option 1 is too much on paper but right on black.

POGGERSemote19
Option 7

Underline grows into a fill

a 2 px rule becomes the whole element

A hairline sits at the bottom edge and grows upward until it has filled the element and inverted the text. It starts as the smallest possible hint and ends as a full inversion, which makes it the natural “succinct” tier — a menu row can stop at the hairline while a button goes all the way.

Whichever you pick becomes hover-wash in src/style.css and every row, menu item, icon button and ghost button in the app follows it. The other two tiers do not change: hover-lift (cards and chips rise) and hover-danger (anything that deletes goes red).
